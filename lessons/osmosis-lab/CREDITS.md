# Credits

Everything in the Osmosis Lab is made in code for this project: the potato, scalpel, paper towel, balance,
beakers, timer and the class board. There are no third-party models, images or sounds, so there is nothing to
licence or credit beyond the project's own licence. three.js (MIT) is loaded from jsDelivr, as in every lesson.

The set-up follows the original Inspired Education EngageVR lesson (cut equal potato pieces with a digital
scalpel, dry and weigh them, place them in beakers at different concentrations, observe over a simulated time
lapse with play/reset controls, then weigh again).

The model: a potato's cells behave as if they held a solution of about 0.32 mol/dm3 of salt (a typical textbook
figure). In anything weaker than that, water moves in and the potato gains mass; in anything stronger, it
loses mass, approaching its final value over a few simulated hours (a simple exponential approach, time
constant about 7 hours, over a simulated 24-hour soak). Each piece varies a little from the average, and an
unevenly cut piece behaves a little differently again, so repeat trials show the kind of scatter a real class
would see.
